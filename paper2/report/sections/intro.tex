\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Receipt key-information extraction (KIE) has converged on two
architectural families.  End-to-end models such as
DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut} produce structured output autoregressively
from the page image at the cost of $\sim\!260$\,M parameters and a
multi-hour fine-tune on a single benchmark.  Modular pipelines such as
PICK~\cite{yu2020pick} retain a detect-then-read-then-assign
decomposition that is cheaper to train but historically loses several
F1 points to the end-to-end family.  This paper asks a narrower
question: \emph{at the canonical SROIE Task-3 evaluation, how close
does the modular pipeline come to the end-to-end model when both arms
are reproduced under their respective original recipes and assigned
identical OCR features?}

We instantiate the modular pipeline as YOLOv8n line
detection~\cite{jocher2023yolov8}, TrOCR-small text
recognition~\cite{li2023trocr}, regex-based field assignment with a
small 3-state header / items / totals hidden Markov zone prior, and
deterministic per-field post-processing.  We deliberately exclude any
learned cross-attention assignment head and any structural verifier
on candidate values: the goal is to establish the engineering
ceiling of a non-learned modular pipeline, not to set state of the
art.  The end-to-end arm is DONUT fine-tuned for 30 epochs at
$1280\times 960$ resolution with the original
\texttt{train\_sroie.yaml} hyperparameters
(\texttt{lr}~$=$~3$\mathrm{e}{-5}$, $\texttt{warmup\_steps}=300$,
batch~size~1, beam~$=$~1).  Both arms read OCR text through the
identical normaliser of \texttt{models/normalize\_bundle.py} so the
comparison is bit-fair (Sec.~\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}  This paper makes two contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A faithful replication study} of end-to-end vs modular
  receipt KIE on the canonical SROIE Task-3 split ($n=347$), with
  paper-recipe DONUT and a regex-and-zone-prior pipeline at
  $\approx\!\VAR{pipeline_params_m}$\,M parameters
  (Tab.~\ref{tab:headline}).  Numbers are reported across $n=5$
  random seeds with paired-bootstrap 95\,\% confidence intervals and
  McNemar's exact test on per-image correctness.
\item \textbf{A catalogue of fourteen silent F1-destroying
  implementation bugs} encountered during reproduction
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:bugs}), each annotated with mechanism, measured F1
  impact, and source-level guard.  Several bugs would degrade DONUT
  to F1\,$<\!0.50$ without producing an error log
  (Bugs~1, 2, 4, 9 in particular); the catalogue functions as a
  regression-test register for any future SROIE-Task-3 reproduction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.}  The modular arm in this
paper is \emph{not} an attempt to set a new SROIE-Task-3 record; it
is the engineering baseline against which architectural extensions
should be measured.  Adding a learned cross-attention assigner, a
structural arithmetic verifier, or other neural components is the
subject of a companion paper that uses the same canonical test split
and reports its results against the same DONUT reference.  We
deliberately keep those extensions out of this manuscript so the
replication boundary stays clean.

\paragraph{Empirical headline.}  On the canonical SROIE Task-3 split,
DONUT under the paper recipe reaches \VAR{donut_f1} macro-F1 (mean
across $n=\VAR{n_trials}$ seeds, paired-bootstrap CI
$[\VAR{donut_bootstrap_ci_lo},\,\VAR{donut_bootstrap_ci_hi}]$).
The modular pipeline reaches \VAR{pipeline_f1}
(\VAR{pipeline_bootstrap_ci_lo},\,\VAR{pipeline_bootstrap_ci_hi}) at
$\approx\!\VAR{pipeline_params_m}$\,M parameters
($\sim\!\VAR{param_ratio_phrase}$ of DONUT's parameter count).  The
paired McNemar exact test on per-image correctness yields
$p=\VAR{mcnemar_p}$.  Per-field decomposition
(Tab.~\ref{tab:perfield}) shows DONUT dominating on \textsf{address}
(long, multi-line spans) and the modular pipeline retaining parity
on \textsf{date} and \textsf{total} (where regex anchors are
strong); the F1 gap concentrates on \textsf{company} and
\textsf{address}, where deterministic rules cannot enumerate the
full distractor set.
