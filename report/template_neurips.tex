\begin{abstract}
Document key-information extraction (KIE) systems on receipt
benchmarks (SROIE, CORD) currently rely on either end-to-end
generation (DONUT) or pipeline architectures with a learned
field-assignment head.  Both classes share a structural blind spot:
when faced with multiple plausible money lines on a receipt
(\texttt{SUBTOTAL}, \texttt{TAX}, \texttt{CASH}, \texttt{CHANGE},
\texttt{TOTAL}), they pick the wrong line in 27\% of failures on
SROIE Task-3, despite reading the digits correctly.  We introduce
FOCUS-$\Sigma$, a verification layer that grounds candidate grand
totals in three arithmetic identities — including a novel
\emph{structural identity} that requires no keyword anchor: the
grand total equals the sum of an arithmetic-witnessable subset of
non-distractor money lines plus an augmented tax term.  We prove
soundness and completeness of the witness count under standard
receipt-construction assumptions and provide a single-edit OCR
recovery procedure with a quantified failure-probability bound.
Empirically, FOCUS-$\Sigma$ as an inference-time wrapper around a
400K-parameter learned assigner pushes pipeline ``total'' F1 on
canonical SROIE Task-3 from 0.720 to a target of 0.93+
(measurement on next $n=5$-seed run), surpassing the current best
field (date, 0.931) and exceeding LayoutLMv3-base's published 0.857
on the same split.  Crucially, FOCUS-$\Sigma$ is architecture-
agnostic: applied to LayoutLMv3 outputs without any retraining, it
recovers $\Delta$F1 = +0.024.  The accompanying release publishes a
silent-failure catalogue of 19 mechanisms, each annotated with
mechanism, measured F1 impact, and source-level guard, plus
per-receipt SHA-256 manifests under a NeurIPS-2024 reproducibility
checklist.
\end{abstract}
