\begin{abstract}
We study whether macro-F1 on the canonical ICDAR-2019 SROIE Task-3
test split ($n=347$) can be lifted by training-set curation alone,
without any architectural change to the DONUT~\cite{kim2022donut}
backbone.  We compare three training regimes: SROIE-only (the
single-corpus baseline), SROIE\,+\,CORD-v2~\cite{park2019cord} (joint
training on a second receipt corpus with a richer schema), and
SROIE\,+\,CORD-v2\,+\,WildReceipt~\cite{sun2021wildreceipt} (joint
training across schema, vendor, and imaging-condition diversity).
The full multi-dataset regime reaches \VAR{donut_f1} macro-F1 on
SROIE Task-3, an absolute lift of $+\VAR{multidataset_delta}$ over
the single-corpus baseline.  The contribution is methodological:
we show that data-curation choices long treated as engineering
decisions are first-class research variables whose effect on F1 is
larger than several architectural modifications recently published
on the same benchmark, and we publish the full training-data
manifest as a reproducibility artefact.
\end{abstract}
