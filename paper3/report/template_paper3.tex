\begin{abstract}
Document key-information extraction (KIE) systems on receipt
benchmarks plateau at $\mathrm{F}_1 \approx 0.83$--$0.86$ across
architectural families.  We argue that the plateau is structural:
modern systems exploit some of the priors a receipt obeys but never
all of them.  We introduce \textbf{SVKIE} (Structure-Verified KIE), a
framework in which five orthogonal structural priors --- arithmetic,
spatial, visual, lexical, and epistemic --- are each encoded as a
distinct module and a unifying verification layer
(\textbf{FOCUS-$\Sigma$}) enforces consistency across priors via three
arithmetic identities.  We prove soundness, completeness, and a
single-edit OCR-recovery probability bound for the verifier; we show
that the structural identity $\mathrm{I}_3$ is keyword-independent and
applies on every receipt satisfying the itemisation hypothesis.  The
framework is architecture-agnostic: applied as an inference-time
wrapper around DONUT, LayoutLMv3-base, and an in-house FOCUS-T head,
it produces a positive wrapper-$\Delta$F1 on every upstream
architecture without any retraining.  On the canonical SROIE Task-3
test set ($n=347$), the FOCUS-T arm under the full SVKIE wrapper
reaches \VAR{pipeline_f1_focus_sigma} macro-F1, an absolute
$+\VAR{ablation_row3_delta_point}$ over the corresponding
single-prior baseline.  Cross-dataset generalisation is demonstrated
on CORD-v2 ($n=\VAR{cord_test_n}$).  We release the framework
implementation and the full SHA-256-pinned reproducibility manifest.
\end{abstract}
